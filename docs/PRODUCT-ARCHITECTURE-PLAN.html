11 November 2026 · Product strategy and proposed architecture

Kitchen From Zero: choose confidently, then cook successfully.

Recommend three meals that fit today's ingredients, preparation state, equipment and time. Build reliability before expanding into an unlimited recipe library.

1. Evidence and strategic decision

This expert audit combines a fresh browser inspection of the public Recipes screen, local source/data inspection and public competitor documentation. It is not a usability study, a kitchen trial or a complete competitor feature inventory. The earlier 64/100 is a historical subjective assessment, not a newly measured score.

The local snapshot contains 109 recipes, 4 with limited external references, 12 preparation pilots and only 7 numeric prerequisite-time ranges. No recipe has a documented kitchen trial. The previous 17 engineering tests do not establish culinary accuracy. The current local work is uncommitted and differs from the public deployment.

Recommendation: make dependable daily decisions the core product. Keep restaurant dishes, street food, café food and festivals in Explore. Recipe breadth is useful only when the instructions are complete and feasible in the user's kitchen. Training a model or adding agents cannot repair missing quantities automatically.

4. A decision in less than 60 seconds

  1. 0–10 seconds: confirm people, time and energy level. Reuse saved preferences. No mandatory account or body measurements.
  2. 10–25 seconds: present three diverse feasible meals: quickest, uses something that needs using, and familiar favourite.
  3. 25–45 seconds: show time range/basis, equipment, ingredient quantities/missing items and one sentence explaining fit.
  4. 45–60 seconds: choose Cook now or Plan for later and confirm any prerequisites. If nothing qualifies, explain the blocker and ask which constraint may change.

Example card copy: “Paneer bhurji · one pan · uses paneer and tomatoes · confirm paneer quantity · no soaking.” Add a time estimate only when supported by recipe data. These example properties are design specimens, not validated catalog claims.

Cooking mode repeats the relevant scaled ingredient amounts per step, shows heat and doneness cues, preserves the current step, provides timers and links components. Scaling quantities does not mean multiplying cooking time. Offer a large-control mode and optional voice navigation later.

Accessibility acceptance checks: keyboard-complete flow, readable focus order, labelled steps, sufficient contrast, reduced motion, 44-pixel primary controls and no horizontal overflow at narrow widths. Verify rather than assuming compliance.

5. Profiles and recommendation algorithms

Progressive onboarding starts with diet, allergies/exclusions, equipment, servings and usual time. Then offer skill, cuisine, spice tolerance, effort/cleanup preference, budget/currency and optional nutrition targets. Support household members with separate preferences and a combined meal context. Allergies are hard constraints, dislikes are preferences.

Kitchen data must include burner count, appliance capacity and fridge/freezer availability. The repository describes a no-fridge kitchen, while some pilot instructions require refrigerated marination: recommendations must detect that conflict. Dry rice and cooked rice are distinct states.

  1. Eligibility: enforce diet, allergen evidence, equipment/capacity, recipe completeness, preparation/storage suitability and upper-bound elapsed time. Unknown safety-critical information cannot support a confident match.
  2. Stock: match canonical ingredients, compatible preparation states and quantity. Unknown quantity means “check stock,” not “ready.” Ordered groceries are not received stock.
  3. Ranking: begin with 30% stock coverage, 20% time slack, 15% use-soon priority, 15% personal preference/history, 10% cost fit and 10% effort fit. These are hypotheses; normalize signals and tune with outcomes. Missing data reduces confidence.
  4. Diversification: return three meaningfully different options; avoid recent repeats unless pinned. Never trade a hard constraint for novelty or popularity.
  5. Explanation: show why a meal qualifies and its uncertainties. Capture dismiss reasons such as “too much effort” or “missing ingredient,” not just likes.

Deterministic rules are enough initially. Personal history can later adjust prep times and preferences. Collaborative filtering needs enough users/interactions; it is unnecessary for a personal prototype. Allow history reset and prevent repetitive recommendations.

Budget estimates need location, price date, package size, edible yield and currency. Show ingredient cost per serving separately from the full-pack cost to buy missing items. Unknown cost must remain unknown.

6. Time savings, leftovers and waste

Model step dependencies and shared resources: soak → grind → cook; chopping may overlap with an unattended wait only if equipment and attention allow. Separate active work, soaking/marinating, preheat, pressure build/release, resting and extra batches. Show elapsed ranges and assumptions; exclude unknown raw-start times from guaranteed quick results.

Pantry lots record amount or uncertainty, bought/opened/prepared dates, storage and optional use-soon priority. Use-soon is not a safety guarantee. Ask about storage before suggesting leftovers. If there is no fridge, disallow plans relying on chilled overnight storage.

Begin planning with three dinners: lock a favourite, swap one meal, reuse compatible ingredients and schedule necessary prep. Shopping sums compatible units, subtracts confirmed stock and leaves uncertain quantities for review. Do not double-allocate one pantry lot to several planned meals. Deduct estimated usage after cooking with user confirmation.

7. Catalog, seasons and food trends

Everyday prioritizes reliable familiar meals; Explore contains restaurant, café, street-food, regional, festival and international collections. Sweets and fried snacks belong in the catalog without automatic healthy labels.

First coverage packs: Karnataka breakfast (Pongal variants, upma, dosa with prepared-batter/from-scratch variants, Kesari Bath, Mangalore buns); snacks/chaat (cabbage pakoda, bhel, sundal); everyday regional curries; café sandwiches/bowls; global dishes with locally available substitutions. Record fermentation, resting, soaking and frying explicitly. Buns and raw-start dosa cannot inherit a quick label from an edited video.

Trend workflow: save creator URL, post/observed dates, platform, region and rights; deduplicate reposts; review quantities, omitted preparation, safety and nutrition; trial the recipe; promote only after review. Keep unreviewed discovery links outside the approved corpus. Do not republish books, captions, images or transcripts without suitable permission. Confirm platform API/licensing feasibility before automation.

Use curated regional seasons and festival calendars first, reviewed annually because dates and harvest seasons vary. Let users select location and festival interests. A viral label requires dated attributable evidence and a freshness window; otherwise use “creator-inspired.” Provide a hide-trends preference.

9. AI features and evaluation

CapabilityImplementation
What can I cook?Parse a question into editable ingredient/time constraints; deterministic matching selects eligible recipes. Start with rules; add language later.
Recipe chatbotRetrieve recipe-version-specific evidence; cite claims or abstain. Keep the existing local evidence-selection adapter as a baseline.
Photo ingredientsSuggest labels and require confirmation. Do not infer reliable weights, freshness, allergens or safety. Prefer local processing when feasible; make remote transfer explicit.
Personal meal plansRules/solver allocate ingredients and equipment; AI explains and accepts language changes without bypassing constraints.
Nutrition analysisCompute from normalized ingredient amounts/yields and cited composition entries; unknown amounts produce incomplete estimates. Recalculate substitutions. LLM prose is not nutrition data.
Adaptive suggestionsUse consented cook history for preparation ranges and preference weights, with reset and explanations.

RAG does not train a model and cooking-only retrieval cannot guarantee accuracy. Build an attributed, permissioned corpus first. Begin with lexical retrieval, add embeddings only if measured recall improves, and evaluate retrieval separately from generation. Multi-agent orchestration is unnecessary until a specific workflow demonstrates benefit.

Evaluation cases must cover servings/quantities, dry versus prepared ingredients, missing steps, equipment incompatibility, unsafe storage, allergens, substitutions, absent/conflicting evidence, stale recipe versions, prompt injection in source content and non-cooking requests. Measure retrieval recall, claim support, abstention, constraint violations, latency and cost. The existing authored evaluation cases have not established real-model performance.

Do not use “diabetic-friendly” as a universal automatic label. Offer reviewed portion/nutrient data and optional user targets; keep treatment advice and lab-based medical inference outside automated recommendations. Existing health content needs a separate review before wider promotion.

11. Architecture and recommended technologies

Keep the working static app while extracting profile, pantry, timing, recipe validation and ranking from the template. Introduce storage and assistant adapters. A full rewrite is not required for Phase 1.

Browser interface
  → profile / pantry / recipe domain services
  → eligibility + ranking + planning
  → local repositories + versioned static catalog
  → optional assistant adapter (local server or cloud API)

Editorial files → schema / evidence review / trials → catalog build
                → static bundle → GitHub Pages

Optional future sync → authenticated API → PostgreSQL + user isolation
                     → revisions / operation log / explicit conflicts

Target stack: TypeScript with Vite for modular frontend code; React only if component/state complexity warrants migration; IndexedDB with Dexie for structured device storage; JSON Schema for portable contracts; Python for editorial/build tools. Retain useful existing Node/Python checks. Pin compatible tool versions and lockfiles when migration starts.

The current standard-library Python server is adequate for local preview and bounded retrieval. Add FastAPI only if API/auth complexity warrants it. A later managed PostgreSQL backend plus one API is sufficient; SQLite suits a separate local editorial tool. No microservices, vector database or Kubernetes are needed for this catalog.

GitHub Pages is static hosting. Python/Ollama, authentication and server-held secrets need a separate service. Core decisions must work without it. With Vite, configure the project base path as /Kitchen/, and test asset paths, service-worker scope and recipe links there. Keep hash routing or generate explicit static recipe pages.

Migrate localStorage through versioned transactions: export first, validate, migrate, verify, then retain rollback support. Never clear data silently on parse failure. Browser storage is origin-bound and may be cleared; local-first does not mean automatic backup or cross-device sync.

Future sync is opt-in: user-isolated access, operation IDs, entity revisions and deletion tombstones. Merge independent cook events; surface conflicting pantry quantities/plans instead of blindly using last-write-wins. Keep private profiles/health records out of Git and default model requests.

13. Working local setup and continuous improvement

Run these commands from your local Kitchen clone. Default preview requires Python 3.10+; tests also need Node 22+. No packages, account or model are required for the current baseline. The PowerShell helper can find this computer's bundled Python.

# From the existing Kitchen repository in PowerShell:
python -X utf8 tools/build_data.py
python -X utf8 tools/build_page.py
python -X utf8 tools/evaluate.py
python -X utf8 tools/validation_audit.py --output ../outputs
.\Start-Local.ps1 -Port 59492
# Open http://127.0.0.1:59492/ ; stop with Ctrl+C

If python is not on PATH, use the same bundled executable selected by Start-Local.ps1 for builds/tests. If the port is occupied/reserved, choose another available fixed port. The default script selects a free port, but changing ports changes browser storage origin; export a backup first. The optional KITCHEN_MODEL adapter requires an existing Ollama installation/model and has not been benchmarked with a real model.

  1. Inspect git status and preserve existing uncommitted work. Create a branch for the next coherent feature when ready; do not reset the clone.
  2. Write acceptance criteria and identify data/contracts before changing UI.
  3. Edit sources/content, rebuild generated files; avoid changes only to index.html.
  4. Run tests and coverage audit. Review affected factual claims and recipes manually; passing engineering tests does not certify cooking.
  5. Preview mobile/desktop and keyboard flows; check focus, empty states, serving changes, links, backup/restore, reload and offline behavior. Test /Kitchen/ subpath hosting before migrating deployment tooling.
  6. Review source/generated diffs and take a local checkpoint commit when desired. Push only when ready; CI should run the same checks. Keep deployment separate and intentional.

Once the target frontend is implemented, define npm run dev/test/build/preview and browser checks in its package manifest. Those are future interfaces, not commands currently available in this repo. Use a lockfile and consistent local/CI runtime versions.

14. Metrics, UX research and release gates

Primary metric: share of sessions producing a feasible accepted meal within 60 seconds, timed from entering Decide including loading and corrections. Also measure time to cooking start and completion so fast clicks cannot hide bad suggestions.

Begin with 5–8 moderated users covering beginners/intermediates, vegetarian/egg/non-vegetarian, one burner, no fridge, unknown pantry quantities and no feasible match. Compare their current method and the prototype on equivalent tasks. This initial sample finds qualitative problems; it cannot establish population-wide performance.

Initial targets—not achieved results: 80% feasible choices within 60 seconds, zero known hard-constraint violations in regression scenarios, complete quantities/dependencies/review records for every promoted recipe, and warm ranking p95 under 200 ms on a specified mid-range phone. Measure cold loading separately. Track abandonment, missing-ingredient surprises, estimate-versus-actual time, cooking success, repetition and optional self-reported waste. No analytics uploads by default.

Vision: a kitchen-aware companion that remembers practical constraints, makes uncertainty visible and improves from real cooking outcomes. The product becomes smarter by helping people finish meals successfully, not by maximizing recipe count or chatbot interactions.
